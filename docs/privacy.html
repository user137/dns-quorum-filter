Privacy policy

Draft, last updated 27 November 2026. It describes the current pre-release version and will be finalised before the Microsoft Store release.

In short

  • The app has no accounts, no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting and no ads. The developer receives nothing from it.
  • To filter, it sends the domain names your browser looks up to the DNS providers you have enabled. That is the one deliberate disclosure, and the settings page names every recipient.
  • Everything else it keeps stays on your computer.

What leaves your computer

Domain names you visit

Only after you agree. On first run the app asks whether it may send domain names to these providers. Until you say yes, it sends none, to anyone: every lookup through the app fails instead. You can withdraw consent at any time on the settings page; sending stops at once. The background connections listed below are not covered by this consent, because they carry no domain names you visit.

When your browser looks up a domain that isn't answered locally (by your allow or block lists, the cache, or another local rule), the app sends that domain name over encrypted DNS-over-HTTPS to:

  • each enabled filtering provider. Out of the box: Quad9 (dns.quad9.net), Cloudflare's malware filter (security.cloudflare-dns.com) and AdGuard DNS (dns.adguard-dns.com). You can turn providers off, add others from the built-in list, or add your own address;
  • an unfiltered reference resolver, used to tell a real answer from a block and to answer when filtering isn't available: Cloudflare (cloudflare-dns.com), with Quad9's unfiltered service (dns10.quad9.net) and Google (dns.google) as fallbacks.

If you add a provider whose address contains your own account ID (NextDNS, Control D or a custom address), that ID goes with every query, as that provider intends.

These providers also see your public IP address, as with any DNS service. Their own privacy policies apply to what they do with it. The app does not send them anything else: no identifier added by the app, no account, no browsing history beyond the lookups themselves.

What stays on your computer

  • Settings (providers, your lists, options) in the app's data folder.
  • The query log (domain, time, decision) is kept in memory and lost when the app stops, unless you turn on saving it. Saved logs and the saved cache are encrypted; the key is in Windows Credential Manager.
  • Personal zone (only if you turn it on): which domains you visit often, stored encrypted with its own key.
  • “Popular sites only” (only while it is on): popular-list domains the filtering providers blocked, so they are dropped from your allowed zone; stored encrypted.
  • Diagnostic logs contain errors and timings, never domain names.
  • The local certificate for 127.0.0.1 is in your user's trusted root store; its private key is in Windows Credential Manager. A MaxMind key, if you enter one, is stored there too.

The settings page and the DNS service listen on 127.0.0.1 only and can't be reached from other devices.

Deleting your data

Tray menu → Remove everything deletes the certificate, all stored keys and credentials, and the app's data folder, then opens Windows Settings so you can uninstall the app. Do this before uninstalling: Windows runs no app code when the package is removed. The log can also be cleared at any time on the settings page.
